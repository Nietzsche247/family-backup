# The DMT Laser Code: Symbol Documentation and Classification Frameworks

The DMT laser code phenomenon—where users report seeing consistent, code-like symbols when viewing a 650nm red laser through DMT’s visual effects—has generated substantial community documentation but **no formal classification system exists**. Danny Goler, who discovered the effect in August 2020,  claims over 1,000 witnesses have seen “the same code,” describing it as katakana-like characters flowing through stable geometric structures.  This report catalogs the reported symbol characteristics, existing documentation methods, and frameworks from linguistics and psychedelic research that could enable systematic cataloging.

## What people actually see: consistent descriptions across experiencers

The phenomenon reportedly consists of **two distinct visual components** that appear when gazing at a diffracted red laser line projected on a wall during low-to-medium dose DMT experiences.

**Rapidly moving character-like forms** dominate experiencer descriptions. Danny Goler compares them to “the cascading digital green script in The Matrix” with “katakana-esque characters.”  One detailed experiencer report describes: “It started off as few points, scattered around, dark-blue I dare say. Then the points started dancing, swirling, and gathering slowly in clumps until they formed very tiny, small, and very thin shapes… They looked like tiny mathematical figures and letters, numbers, and strange other symbols.”   Another participant who tried the protocol with Goler noted: “Whatever unrecognizable characters I saw flowing down were unlike any I’ve ever seen before or after. They weren’t katakana or kanji or anything I recognized.” 

**Stable geometric structures** form the second component, described as “independent of the observer’s frame of reference” and displaying “object permanence”—appearing anchored in physical space rather than floating subjectively. Goler claims the code “goes infinitely into the z-axis” (extending into the surface), though neuroscientist Andrew Gallimore notes this is logically inconsistent since reflected laser light cannot carry information about what lies beyond a surface. 

**Movement characteristics** are frequently mentioned: characters move “so quickly that it’s difficult to ‘catch’ them,”  yet remain stable enough that multiple viewers claim they can “point out specific elements simultaneously, as if examining a physical object.”  The visual quality is often described as “holographic”—one experiencer said “It felt as if I was looking at some kind of a holographic projection that was both inside and outside of the laser.”

**OCR analysis** of laser diffraction videos has detected patterns the algorithms classify as Japanese, Arabic, Hebrew, and English characters—though this likely reflects pareidolia in the software rather than actual script presence. Computational linguist Andrew Gallimore identified actual Japanese katakana characters in community-produced sketches of the phenomenon, suggesting cultural priming may shape what people report seeing. 

**Researchers’ terminology** provides more systematic categorization. Andrés Gómez Emilsson (Qualia Research Institute) describes the patterns as “non-linear, high-frequency resonant modes of the visual field”—framing them as cymatics-like phenomena rather than meaningful symbols.  Gallimore proposes the “laser speckle hypothesis,” where microscopic surface irregularities create interference patterns that act as a “sensory scaffold” for DMT-enhanced pattern recognition. 

## Environmental factors shape the experience systematically

Experiencers naturally track several contextual variables that appear to influence the phenomenon:

**Equipment specifications** are remarkably consistent across reports: **650nm wavelength** (red) specifically, as blue lasers “produce finer, less noticeable speckle patterns” due to shorter wavelength coherence. Line or cross-pattern lasers are required—dot lasers don’t work. Common recommended model: SYD1230 line laser, Class 2, 1-5mW power ($3-5 on Amazon).

**Surface characteristics** demonstrably affect symbol density. One detailed report noted: “On a very ‘soft and smooth’ part of the wall, there were very few characters, hardly ever. On a very ‘rough’ portion of the wall, there were so many!”   This aligns with the laser speckle explanation, since rougher surfaces create more complex interference patterns.

**Viewing technique** requires specific skills: “parallel viewing” (like Magic Eye stereograms), crossing or defocusing eyes, covering one eye, looking “through” rather than at the surface. One protocol describes: “Place 2 fingers against the wall just under the laser line and look at them until you see 3 fingers, then focus your attention on the middle finger.” 

**Dosing parameters** cluster around low-to-medium (“sub-breakthrough”) vaporized DMT doses. Some experiencers report visibility with high-dose psilocybin mushrooms, and MAOIs appear unnecessary for the effect.

## Linguistic frameworks offer rigorous documentation models

The methodology linguists use to approach undeciphered scripts like Linear A, Rongorongo, and Proto-Elamite provides directly applicable frameworks for systematic symbol documentation.

**Core principles from decipherment research:**

1. **No premature interpretation**: Document systematically before assigning meaning. The goal is corpus-building, not immediate translation.
1. **Frequency analysis**: Analyze glyph distributions for Zipf’s law compliance (the pattern natural languages follow where a few symbols appear frequently and many appear rarely). This can distinguish meaningful symbolic systems from random patterns.
1. **Positional analysis**: Track whether forms appear in characteristic positions or sequences—beginnings, endings, or specific combinations.
1. **Co-occurrence patterns**: Document which forms appear together, potentially revealing combinatorial rules.
1. **Multiple independent coders**: Cross-validation of form identification prevents individual bias from shaping the corpus.

**Grapheme analysis from palaeography** provides the technical vocabulary: identify minimal distinctive units (“graphemes”), document allographic variants (same meaning, different form), and catalog ligatures and combinations. The System for Palaeographic Inspections (SPI) characterizes the “calligraphic ideal” for scripts—a model for identifying canonical versus variant forms.

**Computational approaches** now applied to Proto-Elamite and Rongorongo include CNN-based embeddings that map visual forms to high-dimensional vector spaces, enabling similarity measurement via cosine distance. Graph-matching approaches represent symbols as attributed relational graphs for structural comparison. Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) search algorithms (FAISS, Annoy) enable sub-linear retrieval for large catalogs.

## Psychedelic visual research already has substantial classification infrastructure

The broader psychedelic research community has developed mature frameworks for documenting visual phenomena that could scaffold DMT laser code cataloging.

**Heinrich Klüver’s form constants** (1926) remain foundational: four categories (lattices, cobwebs, tunnels, spirals) appear across different users, different psychedelics, and even non-drug conditions like migraine aura.  The Cowan-Ermentrout mathematical model (1979) explains these as products of how the visual field maps onto the visual cortex—they emerge from neural architecture, not external reality. 

**The Subjective Effect Index (SEI)** on PsychonautWiki documents **over 200 effects** with standardized terminology. Its **geometry leveling system** tracks eight intensity levels:

- Levels 1-2: Peripheral visual noise, basic geometric sheen
- Levels 3-4: Translucent overlay becoming complex
- Levels 5-6: Beginning to obscure environment
- Levels 7-8: Complete replacement of visual field with “comprehensive symbolic representations” 

**Lewis-Williams and Dowson’s three-stage trance model** (1988) distinguishes between neurologically universal “entoptic” phenomena and culturally-shaped “construal”— the same geometric form might be interpreted as a tennis racket by one person and a honeycomb by another. This suggests DMT laser code symbols may be universal neural phenomena dressed in culturally-familiar clothing (like Matrix-style katakana).

**Existing databases** provide implementation models:

- **Erowid Experience Vaults**: 117,000+ reports with multi-tier review and 9-level quality ratings
- **Altered States Database (ASDB)**: Open-science database with data from 6,861 reports across 165 journal articles, freely available on OSF
- **Johns Hopkins DMT Entity Survey**: 2,561 participants documenting entity characteristics  with frequency distributions
- **Effect Index replications**: Image and video recreations systematically categorized by effect type

## Building a systematic catalog would require layered classification

A rigorous documentation system for DMT laser code symbols would integrate multiple frameworks:

**Layer 1: Neurological Constants** (universal, following Klüver/Cowan-Ermentrout)

- Catalog form constant categories present (lattice, cobweb, tunnel, spiral)
- Document trigger conditions (dose, substance, set/setting)
- Track which SEI geometry level the experience reaches

**Layer 2: Semiotic Classification** (following Peirce’s categories)

- **Iconic elements**: Visual features resembling external referents (what does this look like?)
- **Indexical elements**: Features caused by physiological states (correlates with dose, etc.)
- **Symbolic elements**: Culturally assigned meanings (Matrix references, etc.) 

**Layer 3: Graphemic Analysis** (borrowing from palaeography)

- Segment visual field into discrete minimal units
- Identify allographic variants across experiencers
- Document ligatures and combinations
- Build sign catalogues analogous to existing script inventories

**Layer 4: Metadata** (following ASDB/Erowid standards)

- Substance, dose, route of administration
- Equipment specifications (laser model, wavelength, power)
- Surface characteristics (texture, color, material)
- Viewing technique employed
- Temporal markers (onset, duration, peak)
- Validated questionnaire responses (5D-ASC, HRS)

**Technical implementation** would require:

- Standardized drawing/description protocols
- Multiple observer reports for inter-rater reliability
- CNN embeddings for visual similarity clustering
- Graph-based representations for complex composite forms
- Version control and audit trails for corpus integrity

## Competing explanations remain unresolved

The fundamental interpretive question remains contested. **Danny Goler’s simulation hypothesis** posits the symbols represent actual code underlying reality—“proof that we live in a simulation.” He claims: “I know whatever it is that we’re looking at is real… whoever is communicating [through ‘the code’] is as real as you and me.” 

**Andrew Gallimore’s laser speckle hypothesis** offers a mundane explanation: microscopic surface irregularities create interference patterns on the retina that serve as a “sensory scaffold” for DMT-enhanced pattern recognition.  The “object permanence” people report—hallucinations appearing stable and anchored—is well-documented in DMT research and doesn’t require external causation. 

**Andrés Gómez Emilsson’s cymatics explanation** frames the phenomenon as “pure physics: the natural behavior of non-linear wave dynamics” in the visual system.  He notes “plenty of people haven’t even seen ‘the code’ despite their best attempts, undermining the claim that the experience has a universal neurovisual signature.”  

**Skeptical replication attempts** have yielded mixed results. Zeus Tipado (University of Maastricht PhD candidate) reported: “I tried it four different times, at different angles, while tripping on DMT, and I didn’t see anything. And I wanted to see it.”   David Luke (Breaking Convention co-founder) saw “a glimmer of a possibility of something code-like emerging on the edge of my perception if I were to fixate on it hard enough” but called it “tantalizing, but strenuous, and massively front loaded with expectation.”  

## Conclusion: documentation infrastructure exists, systematic application does not

The psychedelic research community has developed sophisticated tools for phenomenological documentation—validated questionnaires, standardized effect taxonomies, multi-tier review systems, and open-science databases. Linguistics provides rigorous frameworks for analyzing unknown symbol systems without premature semantic interpretation. What’s missing is systematic application of these tools to the DMT laser code specifically.

A serious cataloging effort would require: **standardized documentation protocols** capturing both symbol characteristics and environmental variables; **multiple independent observers** to establish inter-rater reliability; **computational similarity matching** using modern vision embeddings; and **deliberate agnosticism** about meaning until the corpus is large enough for meaningful pattern analysis. The phenomenon is interesting enough—and the reports consistent enough—to warrant this rigor, regardless of whether the symbols ultimately prove to be neural artifacts or something more surprising.
